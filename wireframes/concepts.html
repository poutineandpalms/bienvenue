Home-screen concepts — Arrival day

Four different answers to the same question: what does a guest see first, what is always within reach, and what waits one level down? All four use the same content (arrival day), so we’re comparing layout, not features. Still grey-box on purpose.

Shared rules: thick black tile = remote focus. Utilities (lights, Goodnight) are the things guests reach for daily; guides and the folio are allowed to live one level down. The hero image is allowed to bleed edge-to-edge — the text sits on top of it.
Concept A — “Cinematic” (Hilton-style)
The photo is the screen. Greeting over the image, one dock of big tiles at the bottom, clock and weather floating top-right. Maximum resort feeling, minimum visible machinery.

Concept B — “One long page” (Netflix-style scroll)
No section pages at all. The hero sits on top and everything lives in shelves below it — guests just keep pressing down. Content appears in journey order: watch, room, explore, stay.
